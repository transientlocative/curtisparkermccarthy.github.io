From the Prologue

Begin at the End

Rance Spurgeon shot Harry Townsend dead in the street at high noon and felt pretty good about it.

Two Pinkerton agents. Twelve years of bad blood. One pair of golden guns Rance had stolen right out of a locked safe — and old Harry Townsend finally got what was coming to him. Rance stood over the body in the dust of that mean street and listened to it not make a sound.

Then Harry Townsend sat up.

Not like a man waking from sleep. Harry Townsend sat up the way a dead man does when something down below has decided it isn't done with him yet. His eyes opened — and they were wrong. The color was wrong. The light behind them was wrong. Everything about Harry Townsend from that moment forward was going to be deeply, permanently, cosmically wrong.

"AND WAR IT WILL BE!" Harry said, in a voice that was his and wasn't. He stood up. He was taller than he'd been a minute ago. He flung open the nearest window and jumped clean out of it into the sky.

Rance Spurgeon stood in the street holding two golden guns and watched his dead partner fly away over the rooftops of the Town With No Name.

"What a world," Rance said.

— Dead Ends, Prologue
